# Rigging plan: the synthetic figure

This is the whole rigging step in one page. Tell me where the direction is
wrong; you don't need to answer anything part by part.

## In short

I'll give the figure a light skeleton that keeps the painted pose readable:
the body sways and breathes, the head tilts, the arms swing a little, and the
hair and scarf trail behind the movement. The face keeps the painted view.
Nothing will move so far that the painting starts to look redrawn.

## The head keeps the painted view

The head tilts and nods but does not turn toward or away from you. The
painting shows the face almost straight on, and turning it would mean inventing
the side of the face that was never painted.

## The skeleton

- One chain runs from the hips up through the chest to the neck and head, so
  the upper body can lean and breathe as one piece.
- Each arm gets three bones (upper arm, forearm, hand), so an elbow can bend
  without the sleeve folding at the wrong place.
- The legs stay still. The figure is standing, and moving the feet would make
  it look like it is slipping on the ground.
- The hair gets two short chains on each side and the scarf one chain, so they
  can swing after the body moves.

## How the parts bend

- The upper body, sleeves and scarf bend softly, because they are cloth and
  should fold smoothly.
- The hands, the face and the hair ornament stay rigid. Bending them would
  smear the painted detail.
- The face gets a finer grid than the rest so the eyes can blink cleanly; the
  rest uses a coarse grid to keep the rig light.

## What follows the movement

- The hair and the scarf follow the body with a short delay and settle on their
  own, like light cloth in still air.
- The delay is gentle. A strong swing would look like wind, and the painting
  has no wind in it.
- The elbows stop before they bend backwards, and the head stops before the
  neck looks stretched.

## What I'm leaving out

- No leg or foot movement.
- No head turn, as above.
- No new parts: I'll only rig what the parts step produced.

## Where your view matters most

1. Is a light sway the right energy, or should the figure feel more alive?
2. Should the scarf move as much as the hair, or stay calmer?
